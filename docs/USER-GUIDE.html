01What it is

Wild Sync fires two Sony ILX-LR1 cameras at the same instant — inside a couple of thousandths of a second of each other — and writes down where the boat was, which way the rig was pointing, and exactly when each shutter opened. That is what turns two piles of photographs into a 3D model of the seafloor.

You drive all of it from one page on the rig Mac: http://localhost:9090. There is nothing else to run. If that page is up and the header says 2/2 cameras, the rig is ready to shoot.

The three machines

Worth knowing which is which, because when something breaks the fix is usually specific to one of them.

machinerunsowns
the Macrigdthe brain: settings, the shot schedule, pulling pictures, writing transect folders, serving this page
cam1 / cam2ilxctl + piagenta Raspberry Pi beside each camera: talks to the body over USB, and fires it down a wire
the bodies—the cameras themselves. The card holds the full-quality RAW; only a small review JPEG crosses to the Mac during a run
The one design rule

Both cameras must see the world the same way, or the pair is useless for photogrammetry. So exposure, white balance and recording format are pushed to both bodies and read back to prove they took. Focus and zoom are the deliberate exception — they stay per-camera, because the two lenses' position counts have never been proven to mean the same thing, and copying one to the other could silently change the geometry of your stereo pair.

Never

The rig never uses autofocus, on any path. It is a fixed survey rig; focus is set by hand and left alone. The software refuses AF rather than trusting you not to pick it by accident.

If the page goes grey

A RIG LINK LOST banner means the browser has stopped hearing from the Mac. Every number on screen is frozen from that moment, and the banner says how old it is. The rig itself may be shooting perfectly well — the page recovers on its own when the link comes back.

Six tabs: Review (drive the survey), Transects (browse what you shot), Controls (camera settings), Nav, IMU, and Events (the rig's diary).

03Before you dive

  1. Power, and both cameras present

    Injector on for cam1, switch on for cam2. Wait for 2/2 cameras in the header.

    cam1 stays on its own PoE injector. Two cameras firing together overran the switch's power budget and it dropped a port every time — that was measured, not guessed.

  2. The Mac's clock

    System Settings → General → Date & Time → Set time and date automatically, on.

    This matters more than it sounds. The Mac's clock is the reference every shot is scheduled against. Left undisciplined it drifts — this one was found 187 ms out and stepping — and the cameras stop firing on schedule and start firing whenever the message arrives. The rig now compensates for the offset and warns you about it, but the honest fix is a disciplined clock.

  3. The camera clocks

    Set date and time in each camera's own menu. USB cannot do it — the body refuses.

    Not fatal if you skip it: every frame is timestamped from the hardware exposure signal, not the camera's clock. It just keeps the card's own filenames sensible.

  4. Cards

    Fast cards only — V60 / UHS-II. Check there is no card_missing or body_locked alert.

  5. Settings

    Controls tab. The fleet line says what both bodies are meant to be on; each camera's own column shows what it actually reports back. They should agree.

  6. Nav

    For live GPS the iKonvert needs all four DIP switches ON (RAW mode) — power-cycle it after changing them.

    Without it, arm a static position instead. The header then reads nav STATIC so you are never fooled into reading one fixed point as a live track.

  7. Disk

    The disk pill covers the Mac's transect volume. A review JPEG is ~200 KB; the RAW that stays on the card is ~32 MB and needs room in ~/rig-raw when you drain it.

  8. Focus

    Focus each camera by eye in Controls, then leave it. The two bodies will show different focus numbers — that is expected and correct.

04Shooting a transect

In Review: type a label, set the interval in seconds, set how many frames (0 = until you stop), leave auto-capture ticked, press Start transect.

Three kinds of line, and that is the whole safety model:

  • Green — fine.
  • Amber — a warning. The run will record anyway; Start anyway commits.
  • Red — blocked. It will not start until you fix it.

While it runs

  • The jitter chip between the two frames is the measured gap between those two exposures — not an estimate. Green is good; the budget is 10 ms.
  • If one camera has no frame for a shot you get a MISSING panel in its place. You are never shown a single frame dressed up as a pair.
  • ◀ older / newer ▶ or the arrow keys step through pairs; following newest snaps back to live.
  • The strip under each camera is that node's health: card state, battery, disk, exposure edges seen, and its clock offset if it drifts.
If a camera dies mid-run

The run pauses. It does not carry on shooting the other camera alone — a lone frame is not a stereo pair, and it would quietly pollute the transect. You will see capture_paused. It resumes by itself once the camera is back and has proven it can actually fire, not merely that it is answering.

Capture one takes a single synchronised pair without starting a run — the quick way to check focus and exposure.

Stopping

Hold the Stop button for a full second. The bar fills as you hold; a tap does nothing. Stopping finalises the transect and, by default, starts the card drain. Give it up to 20 seconds — it is finishing the pulls that are in flight.

05Getting the RAWs off the cards

During a run the full-quality RAW never crosses the USB cable — it cannot, at survey speed. It stays on the camera's card and comes off between runs.

For every shot the sequence is: pull it to the Mac → check the copy is byte-for-byte identical → only then delete it from the card. A shot means both its files, the RAW and the card JPEG; neither is deleted until both are verified on the Mac. If anything goes wrong — a bad checksum, a dropped link, the Mac's disk filling — the whole shot stays on the card.

The rule that does not bend

Nothing is ever deleted from a card until a verified copy exists on the Mac. Cancelling a drain is safe for exactly this reason: it stops between shots, never between a verify and a delete.

The camera cannot shoot while draining, so the rig refuses to start a run during a drain, and Start will tell you so rather than just bouncing.

Afterwards ingest renames the RAWs into the rig's naming scheme and writes a sidecar beside each one carrying the true capture time, position and attitude — so the RAWs carry their metadata into whatever you process them with.

"Card index not ready"

That body's transfer subsystem is wedged — it happens after many mode switches in a session. Power-cycle that camera and drain again. The card is untouched and nothing is lost. The rig will not keep retrying a wedged body after every run; it tells you and waits.

06The flight log

Each camera gets a flight_log.csv per transect: one row per frame, 23 columns — filename, UTC time, position, depth, attitude, heading, accelerometer, gyro, temperature, and three columns that tell you how much to trust the rest.

Empty means empty

A blank cell means the rig did not have that value. It never invents one.

The column to read first

capture_source says how the time in that row was obtained:

valuemeaningtrust
gpio_edgethe camera's own EXPOSURE signal, timestamped by the Pi's kernelbest — this is what the harness is for
exifthe camera's internal clock, corrected by a measured per-body offsetgood to ~10 ms
commandwhen the rig asked for the shot, not when it happenedtreat with suspicion

A healthy transect is gpio_edge the whole way down. The companion column time_err_ms carries the honest error bar on that instant — including the uncertainty in translating the camera node's clock to the Mac's. It is small, but it is no longer pretended to be zero.

07Settings

Exposure — per camera, until you say otherwise

Change aperture, shutter or ISO on one camera and it happens on that camera, live, while you watch its picture. When you want both the same, press Apply exposure to fleet — it copies the exposure from the camera named on the button.

Leaving the two deliberately different is a legitimate thing to do — balancing uneven lighting between the bodies — so the page shows "bodies differ" as a note, not an error. Nothing else you do will quietly undo that balance.

Focus and zoom — this camera only, always

There is no fleet button, on purpose (see §01). Hold to drive; the slider is the lens's own position count. Lens details live in each section's header.

Recording format — pushed to every camera

File type, image size, what gets sent to the Mac, RAW compression, white balance. Each camera then reports back what it actually accepted, so you can see both bodies really are recording the same thing rather than trusting the message got through. Before this pass those fields were write-only and the rig was guessing.

White balance is pinned to a fixed colour temperature deliberately: auto white balance renders the two bodies differently shot to shot, which fights both stereo matching and any colour work later.

Anything the camera will only let you change on the body itself is listed under "set on the body only", rather than the page pretending it can drive it.

08When it goes wrong

Every alert names what happened and what to do. The ones you are most likely to meet, in that order:

A camera vanished

node_offline, camera_absent, ilx_down. If the Pi answers but the camera does not, the camera daemon is wedged: power-cycle the camera body. Shots still fire; pictures will not arrive.

A Pi rebooted

node_rebooted, node_undervoltage. That is power, not software — a Pi does not restart on its own. Check the injector and the switch. Frames fired while it was down are gone.

The camera looks fine but records nothing

body_locked, card_write_stuck. This is the card stall, and it is the nastiest failure this rig has. One frame the card will not accept jams the whole body: settings lock up, delivery stops, format is refused — and the shutter still fires, so it looks exactly like a software bug. Power the body fully off, take the card out, full-format it in a computer, format it again in the camera. If it argues, replace it.

The two cameras disagree

settings_divergent. They are not identically configured, so pairs taken now are not comparable. Usually the body is sitting on its own menu, or has taken control priority back.

Clocks

host_clock_offset, node_clock_skew. The whole sync budget is 10 ms and clock disagreement spends it directly. Host offset: turn on automatic time on the Mac. Node skew: the two Pis need to agree with each other.

Measured but not alarmed

Three things the rig shows without raising a pill: the IMU's attitude rate (about 50 Hz, below the 60 Hz target), a GPS time source two senders disagree about, and a FOCUS line held longer than a calibration should hold it. Read them in the IMU tab and in Events rather than waiting for an alert.

10Recovery

First thing to try

Power-cycling the camera body fixes a surprising share of problems: a wedged daemon, a stuck transfer session, a locked property table. It is safe, and it is faster than diagnosing.

# what the fleet thinks
curl -s localhost:9090/api/fleet | python3 -m json.tool
curl -s localhost:9090/api/anomalies | python3 -m json.tool

# a node's own health
curl -s 192.168.1.201:8081/health | python3 -m json.tool

# restart the brain — finalises any active run first, nothing is lost
launchctl kickstart -k gui/$(id -u)/org.wildtechnology.wildsync.rigd

# clear a Pi's leftover picture spool
curl -s -X POST 192.168.1.201:8080/api/spool/prune -d '{"confirm":"prune"}'

11Limits

  • No strobe yet. The wiring is in and the software supports it, but it has never been fired in anger.
  • The IMU runs at about 50 Hz, not the 60 Hz originally wanted. The page reports the real rate rather than a nicer one.
  • cam1's power stays on its own injector.
  • No fleet focus or zoom, deliberately, until lens-encoder parity is proven.
  • Delivered JPEGs are review-size (~1.7 MP). The survey resolution lives in the RAW on the card — drain it.
